Arusha Tanzania Agricultural Project

Arusha Tanzania Agricultural Project Tanzania’s agricultural landscape is rapidly shifting toward commercialization, mechanization, and climate resilience. Key ongoing initiatives include the rollout of 10,000 tractors to boost mechanization, massive horticulture expansion in the Southern Highlands targeting a $2B export market, and strategic land reclamation projects

Tanzania’s agricultural sector is undergoing a massive transformation with major projects focusing on youth empowerment, climate adaptation, mechanization, and value addition. Key ongoing programs include the Building a Better Tomorrow (BBT) youth initiative and the Southern Agricultural Growth Corridor (SAGCOT) Government & National ProgramsBuilding a Better Tomorrow (BBT - Youth Initiative): A flagship government program targeting youth and women. The Ministry acquired over 340,000 acres across regions like Dodoma, Kigoma, Mbeya, and Singida to create commercial block farms, modern agricultural parks, and incubate next-generation agribusinesses.Agricultural Sector Development Programme Phase II (ASDP II): A nationwide framework focused on transforming crops, livestock, and fisheries into highly productive, commercialized systems that guarantee food security.Agricultural Inputs Support Project: Backed by the African Development Bank, this project boosts crop yields (focusing on wheat, sunflower, and rice) in key zones, directly benefiting 1.2 million smallholder households with mechanization and quality inputs.

"Agriculture is our wisest pursuit, because it will in the end contribute most to real wealth, good morals, and happiness"

Agriculture remains a cornerstone of Tanzania’s economy, contributing approximately 27 percent to the country’s Gross Domestic Product (GDP) and employing nearly 75 percent of the workforce. The sector plays a crucial role in food security and exports, with recent government initiatives focusing on modernization, digitalization, infrastructure development, and financing to enhance productivity and market access. Tanzania’s economy recorded 5.1 percent real GDP growth in the 2023/24 fiscal year, with agriculture being a key contributor.

Tanzania Livestock Farming Project

Tanzania’s livestock sector is undergoing rapid transformation, driven by a push to improve genetics, boost milk and meat production, and adopt climate-smart practices. Key initiatives include the Tanzania Livestock Master Plan, the World Bank's Climate-Smart Livestock interventions, and local capacity-building programs. It has catalyzed domestic milk production and fostered the growth of local abattoirs

Tanzania boasts the second-largest cattle population in Africa (over 36 million). To tap into this, the government and private investors are actively driving modernization and commercialization through the TISEZA investment portal. Notable initiatives under these plans feature

Integrated Ranching and Meat Processing: New large-scale ventures (such as feedlots and slaughterhouses) target locations like Morogoro to expand high-quality halal meat production for domestic and export markets

Dairy Innovation Centers: Projects such as MiL-ANIC (often supported by national youth programs) are working to boost milk production, train farmers, and increase forage and pasture seed availability across multiple regions

Sector Financing: The government has heavily funded livestock disease control, artificial insemination, and the construction of vital infrastructure like livestock ports and markets to increase sector productivity

"Mifugo ni Mali: Tutunze Tulinde. (Livestock is wealth: let's care and protect.)"

The livestock population is highly concentrated in specific regions due to climate and cultural traditions. Over 70% of the cattle are found across eight primary regions

Tanzania Irrigation Farming Project

Tanzania’s irrigation sector relies primarily on surface (furrow) systems, particularly for rice paddies, alongside increasing adoption of modern drip and solar-powered systems to combat climate variations. The government has set a target of reaching 1.2 million hectares of irrigation coverage. National oversight and scheme registrations are managed by the National Irrigation Commission (NIRC)

Irrigation is vital to Tanzania's agricultural sector, accounting for the bulk of national freshwater withdrawals. With over 1.5 million hectares of potentially irrigable land, the country relies on these systems to mitigate climate variability and boost yields for key crops like rice, maize, and horticultural products.

"Kilimo cha Umwagiliaji: Uhakika wa Chakula na Utajiri Endelevu!"

For local, on-the-ground support, farmers can access modern water-saving setups and technologies through Balton Tanzania. Additionally, adopting improved techniques can be guided by frameworks like Modern Irrigation in Tanzania: A Key to Sustainable Agriculture.

Tanzania Ranching Farming Project

Tanzania’s livestock sector is undergoing a massive shift from traditional pastoralism to commercial ranching, heavily backed by government and World Bank initiatives to boost meat exports and generate rural wealth. Commercial ranching focuses primarily on beef, dairy, and leather production, with major operations concentrated in the Dodoma, Morogoro, Coast, and Kagera regions

The Tanzania Ranching Farming Project generally refers to large-scale, commercial livestock and agricultural development initiatives designed to modernize meat production, boost export capacity, and prevent land degradation. These projects often involve private-public partnerships with the government.

"Commercialization and genetic improvement of the livestock industry"

Farmers today face mounting challenges from climate change, including erratic rainfall, droughts, floods, and rising temperatures that threaten yields and food security. Conventional farming practices often worsen the problem by depleting soils, reducing biodiversity, and increasing greenhouse gas emissions. With global food demand rising, these pressures risk deepening hunger and poverty if not addressed through sustainable solutions.

Land Reclamation Agriculture Project

Land reclamation in Tanzania focuses primarily on agricultural expansion, soil degradation reversal, and protecting coastal areas from sea-level rise. The government prioritizes nature-based restoration and international partnerships to revitalize degraded lands into productive areas, supported by the National Land Use Planning Commission.

Tanzania is advancing large-scale land reclamation and agriculture projects to combat degradation, which costs the economy roughly US$10.1 billion annually. These key initiatives are detailed below:

Egypt-Tanzania Agricultural Partnership: Tanzania and Egypt are advancing joint memoranda of understanding focused on large-scale agricultural land reclamation, livestock development, and the establishment of dedicated logistics zones to enhance food security.

LDFS Program: Backed by the International Fund for Agricultural Development (IFAD), the Reversing Land Degradation Trends and Increasing Food Security (LDFS) project targets 12,950 households across Kondoa, Nzega, Mkalama, Magu, and Micheweni. It focuses on reversing degradation in semi-arid ecosystems.

"Restore the land, revive the earth" or "Reclaiming land, restoring life"

What is the agriculture program in Tanzania?
