Arusha Tanzania Agricultural Project

Tanzania’s agricultural landscape is rapidly shifting toward commercialization, mechanization, and climate resilience. Key ongoing initiatives include the rollout of 10,000 tractors to boost mechanization, massive horticulture expansion in the Southern Highlands targeting a $2B export market, and strategic land reclamation projects

Tanzania Livestock Farming Project

Tanzania’s livestock sector is undergoing rapid transformation, driven by a push to improve genetics, boost milk and meat production, and adopt climate-smart practices. Key initiatives include the Tanzania Livestock Master Plan, the World Bank's Climate-Smart Livestock interventions, and local capacity-building programs. It has catalyzed domestic milk production and fostered the growth of local abattoirs

Tanzania Irrigation Farming Projects

Tanzania’s irrigation sector relies primarily on surface (furrow) systems, particularly for rice paddies, alongside increasing adoption of modern drip and solar-powered systems to combat climate variations. The government has set a target of reaching 1.2 million hectares of irrigation coverage. National oversight and scheme registrations are managed by the National Irrigation Commission (NIRC)

Tanzania Ranching Farming Project

Tanzania’s livestock sector is undergoing a massive shift from traditional pastoralism to commercial ranching, heavily backed by government and World Bank initiatives to boost meat exports and generate rural wealth. Commercial ranching focuses primarily on beef, dairy, and leather production, with major operations concentrated in the Dodoma, Morogoro, Coast, and Kagera regions

Land Reclamation Agriculture Project

Land reclamation in Tanzania focuses primarily on agricultural expansion, soil degradation reversal, and protecting coastal areas from sea-level rise. The government prioritizes nature-based restoration and international partnerships to revitalize degraded lands into productive areas, supported by the National Land Use Planning Commission.
