Arusha Region Climatic Project

  • Admin
  • Updated

In Arusha urban, northern Tanzania, groundwater contributes about 80% of the water supply. However, elevated fluoride levels and evidence of anthropogenic pollution have been reported in the groundwater around Mount Meru which is a water source for Arusha urban. This study aims at understanding the recharge areas and flow pathways of groundwater in what has been a poorly monitored area.

In Arusha, the wet season is overcast and warm and the dry season is windy, comfortable, and partly cloudy. Over the course of the year, the temperature typically varies from 56°F to 83°F and is rarely below 51°F or above 88°F.

Manyara Region Climatic Project

In the Tarangire-Manyara ecosystem, extreme weather variations, exacerbated by climate change. Implemented by CARE Tanzania across Kiteto and Simanjiro districts, it focuses on Ecosystem-based Adaptation (EbA) to mitigate water scarcity, drought, and land-use conflicts among pastoralists and farmers. this multi-million dollar project targets severe land degradation, sediment buildup, and the impacts of erratic weather on Lake Babati.

The Manyara Climate Projects encompass multiple ecological and community initiatives aimed at restoring degraded savannas and mitigating the severe impacts of climate change in the Tarangire-Manyara ecosystem. Key actions include removing unpalatable bush encroachment, rehabilitating thousands of acres of pasture, and empowering local women as promoters of sustainable land management

Hanang District Disaster Recovery: Climate Hub Tanzania has implemented post-disaster restoration in the region's Hanang District following severe El Niño-induced floods and landslides, focusing on repairing homes, latrines, and local resilience.

Manyara-Tarangire Ecosystem Project: Run by the Oikos Institute and partners, this pilot focuses on training women in pastoralist communities to promote sustainable pasture management and mitigate the economic risks of extreme weather variations

"Voices of Manyara: Living the Climate Reality, Leading the Change"

Several climate resilience and ecosystem restoration initiatives are currently underway in the Tarangire-Manyara ecosystem. Key efforts focus on climate-resilient community programs, habitat restoration to clear invasive bush encroachment, and sustainable groundwater management to protect wildlife and local livelihoods

Kilimanjaro Region Climatic Project

Several major climate and environmental initiatives are active in the Kilimanjaro region, focusing on glacial melt, water security, and deforestation. Key projects include the UNESCO-FAO Kilimanjaro Water Tower Project, The Kilimanjaro Project, and continuous mountain climate tracking by GEO Mountains Climate Tracking on the long-term scientific monitoring of Mount Kilimanjaro’s climate.

Key Climate Projects & Initiatives

Elevation-Dependent Climate Change Project: A 20-year initiative tracking a 22-station transect spanning the mountain's full gradient (900–5,800m) to model temperature shifts, glacier decay, and precipitation patterns.

Kilimanjaro Water Tower Project: An $8 million investment by UNESCO and the FAO to map shared aquifers, improve dry-season groundwater storage, and restore 400 km² of vital cloud forest.

Sustainable Land Management (SLM): Regional efforts partnering with local Chagga and Maasai communities to combat deforestation, promote joint forest management, and develop alternative incomes.

"Empowering youth to become local champions for climate adaptation."

Climate fluctuations heavily impact the Chagga homegardens on the southern and eastern slopes, where shifting temperatures alter optimal elevations for coffee and banana yields

Several climate initiatives and environmental projects operate in the Moshi area of Kilimanjaro, Tanzania, targeting the local impacts of climate change like shifting weather patterns, deforestation on Mount Kilimanjaro, and water scarcity. Because "Moshi area Climatic Project" can refer to a few different collaborative efforts, the major ongoing and recent initiatives are detailed below

"From Dust to Green"

The hot season lasts for 2.7 months, from January 5 to March 28, with an average daily high temperature above 88°F. The hottest month of the year in Moshi is February, with an average high of 90°F and low of 68°F. The cool season lasts for 3.1 months, from May 21 to August 25, with an average daily high temperature below 81°F. The coldest month of the year in Moshi is July, with an average low of 61°F and high of 79°F.

Ngorongoro Area Climatic Project

The Ngorongoro Conservation Area Authority oversees climate change adaptation,focusing on a large-scale planting program. To offset carbon emissions and reduce pressure on the Northern Highland Forest Reserve, the NCAA has planted over 2.6 million trees across bordering districts including Karatu and Arumeru.

Multiple climate and environmental projects are active in and around the Ngorongoro Conservation Area (NCA). To explore or participate in these efforts, see the Ngorongoro Conservation Area Authority for conservation details and the Tanzania Natural Resource Forum for local adaptation programs.

These programs center around several primary environmental goals to balance preservation and local livelihoods:

Reforestation & Carbon Reduction: The NCAA operates major tree-planting programs targeting species like Cordia africana, Acacia, and Eucalyptus, with active tree nurseries located in nearby Karatu, Mbulu, Lositete, and Selela.

Biodiversity Resilience: Initiatives like the UNDP's Building Resilience of Forest Biodiversity Against the Threats of Climate Change focus on the northern highlands (including Arusha) to secure ecosystems and mitigate ecological risks.

"We serve for Sustainable Conservation and Tourism Excellence"

Pilot studies focus on mainstreaming climate adaptation into dryland development in the Ngorongoro and Monduli districts, aiming to balance traditional Maasai pastoralist land management with modern climate-resilient practices.

Marangu Area Climatic Project

The Marangu area (located at roughly 2000 m elevation) serves as a primary access point for Mount Kilimanjaro and features distinct climatic projects and microclimate characteristics. Marangu features a humid, temperate mountain climate. Daytime temperatures generally range from 22°C to 28°C (72°F to 82°F).

For a broader look at Tanzania's national adaptation strategies, the country is executing a nationwide Integrating Climate Change Adaptation effort supported by the UN. Furthermore, community groups like the Tanzania Association of Environmental Engineers have led similar resilience projects in northern Tanzanian districts

""Keeping Grasslands Healthy"

To preserve this ecosystem, Tanzania National Parks (KINAPA) actively runs ecological projects in the Marangu area focused on reforestation and community tree nurseries. Furthermore, high-altitude physiology and agroforestry research centers document how locals manage climate-resilient Chagga homegardens to adapt to these weather extremes.
